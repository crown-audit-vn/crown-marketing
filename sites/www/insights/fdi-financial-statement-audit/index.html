Financial statement audits for FDI enterprises in Vietnam

Many foreign-invested enterprises in Vietnam are required to have their annual financial statements audited by an independent auditor. This article explains, in general terms, what that engagement usually covers and how companies can prepare.

Crown Insights·7 min read
In this articleWhat the audit is forPoints that often need early attentionHow to prepare

Under Vietnamese regulations on independent audit and enterprise reporting, foreign-invested enterprises are commonly required to have their annual financial statements audited by an independent audit firm. The exact obligation depends on the company’s legal form and the rules in force for the reporting year. This overview is general and is not a legal opinion on any one entity.

What the audit is for

An independent audit of the financial statements provides users — including owners, lenders, and in many cases regulators — with an auditor’s report on whether the statements are prepared, in all material respects, in accordance with the agreed financial reporting framework. For FDI companies, that framework is often Vietnamese Accounting Standards or another basis required for the entity, as agreed in the engagement.

The audit is not the same as a tax finalization, a review, or agreed-upon procedures. Those engagements have different objectives and different levels of assurance, as described on Crown Audit’s pages for annual financial statement audits and reviews and agreed-upon procedures.

Points that often need early attention

FDI companies frequently need to align the chart of accounts and supporting documents with the reporting framework, keep related-party balances and transactions clearly documented, and make sure opening balances, capital contributions and intra-group charges can be traced. Differences between management reports and statutory financial statements should be reconcilable before the audit starts.

Where the company also has tax or transfer pricing work in progress, the financial statement file and the tax file should tell a consistent story. Crown discusses related tax topics in tax compliance for FDI enterprises and CIT finalization.

How to prepare

Preparation usually means closing the books on time, assembling contracts and invoices for significant balances, and appointing the auditor early enough to agree scope, timetable and the reporting framework. A short discussion of unusual transactions, new entities or changes in accounting policy at the start of the engagement can reduce delays later.

For a broader view of what an audit contributes beyond the signed report, see financial statement audit: beyond the compliance signature. To discuss a specific engagement, contact Crown Audit.
